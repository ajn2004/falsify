# Experimental protocol: V0 and V0.1

**Status:** V0 research-design document; V0.1 quantities remain to be preregistered.
**Scope:** Experimental design and scientific constraints, not an implementation specification.

## Research question

Under a fixed intervention budget, can an LLM policy select experiments that identify or predict a hidden dynamical system more effectively than non-adaptive controls, when evaluated on worlds and observations not used to tune that policy?

The question is falsifiable: the LLM may outperform, match, or underperform the controls. Evaluation concerns recorded choices, outputs, and outcomes, not inferred private reasoning or claims about internal cognitive mechanisms.

## V0 and V0.1 task

V0 establishes the controlled apparatus and its information boundary. V0.1 asks whether an LLM scientist's adaptive experiment choices yield useful parameter inference and/or held-out prediction for a hidden damped harmonic oscillator, relative to seeded-random and fixed-design policies.

A canonical model for communicating the physical task is

```text
x''(t) + 2 ζω₀ x'(t) + ω₀² x(t) = F(t)
```

where `x` is displacement, `ζ` is a damping ratio, `ω₀` is an undamped natural angular frequency, and `F(t)` denotes external forcing under the environment's declared units and convention. The equation is a task description, not a complete simulator specification. The exact parameter ranges and distributions, forcing family, initial-condition domain, observation schedule, numerical solver and tolerances, and inference target remain open until specified and justified in the relevant implementation/protocol-lock work. The environment must define them consistently and preserve evaluator-only truth.

The V0.1 estimand is the difference in preregistered performance between policies under matched worlds, budgets, and observation conditions. Whether the primary endpoint is parameter inference, held-out prediction, or a declared combination remains to be decided in preregistration; it must not be selected after inspecting confirmatory outcomes.

## Experimental units and terminology

- **Hidden world:** One generated oscillator instance, including its fixed physical truth and any world-level configuration. A world is evaluator-owned; a seed is not itself an agent-visible world descriptor.
- **Experiment/intervention:** One valid, budget-consuming request that sets permitted controllable conditions and receives a measurement according to the protocol. The allowed controls and their ranges are implementation choices that must be documented before evaluation.
- **Observation:** The returned public measurement, including only protocol-declared measured values and measurement metadata. A noisy observation is generated by the declared observation process; clean means exactly zero intentionally added measurement noise.
- **Agent decision:** One externally observable policy output at a decision point, such as a proposed next intervention, a permitted repeat/stop choice, or a final estimate/prediction when requested. The protocol records outputs, not private chain-of-thought.
- **Complete run:** One policy execution for one world, condition, and budget, with its action/observation history, terminal status, final reported outputs, and evaluator-scored outcomes.
- **Repeat:** A distinct policy execution under a declared repeat identifier. For stochastic models, repeats distinguish sampling variability; repeated runs must not be silently merged or treated as independent worlds.
- **Condition:** A preregistered combination of policy, observation-noise regime, and intervention budget (plus any explicitly declared model/run settings). V0.1 does not include system-one assistance.

## Information and control boundary

### Policy-visible public state

At each decision, the scientist policy may receive only information a participant could legitimately obtain under the protocol:

- the task description and public model family, including units/conventions explicitly declared to participants;
- the currently permitted action schema, valid control bounds, remaining budget, and protocol-defined action/measurement rules;
- its own prior actions and the observations actually returned to it in this run;
- public status needed to continue the experiment, such as whether an action was accepted and the number of interventions remaining;
- its own prior structured decisions and, if part of the declared protocol, its own previous estimates.

Any policy-visible metadata must be audited for indirect encoding of truth or condition labels. The protocol must enumerate the actual public-state fields before model evaluation. Noise-regime labels are not automatically public: reveal them only if the participant protocol explicitly grants that knowledge, and apply the same declared information to all policies.

### Evaluator-only state

The evaluator may access hidden physical parameters, clean trajectories, held-out targets, generation metadata, condition assignments not disclosed to the policy, and scoring data. Such state is used only for outcome generation or post-run evaluation and must not be serialized into the policy context or shared mutable objects reachable by the policy.

### Simulator internals

Solver state, internal object representations, debug traces, file paths, exception details, world/noise seeds, and evaluator implementation details are not participant observations. A controlled public error response may indicate action acceptance or protocol validity, but must not expose hidden values or internal diagnostics.

## Independent variables and conditions

V0.1 must vary at least:

1. **Experimental policy:** an LLM scientist, seeded-random selection, and a predeclared fixed non-adaptive design. All receive the same public information and action opportunities.
2. **Observation-noise condition:** clean observations with exactly zero intentionally added measurement noise and one or more preregistered noisy conditions. Noise family, scale, and construction remain to be established and frozen; the project guidance identifies seeded Gaussian noise as the starting point, not an already quantified regime.
3. **Intervention budget:** a fixed maximum number of budget-consuming experiments per run. The values and whether multiple budgets are studied remain TBD for preregistration.

Policy/model version, prompts, sampling parameters, and other model settings are controlled configuration and must be held fixed within a confirmatory condition or explicitly varied as preregistered factors. World variation and repeats are design dimensions, not substitutes for the condition factors above.

**Not a V0.1 condition:** system-one/fast-model advice. It remains disabled. Its effect belongs to a later experiment (planned V0.3); no benefit is assumed.

## Dependent variables

Outcomes are measured from observable run records and evaluator-scored artifacts:

- **Parameter-estimation performance:** closeness/calibration of final reported parameter estimates to hidden truth, if parameter estimates are required by the selected task.
- **Held-out predictive performance:** error of predictions on protocol-designated data not returned during experimental decisions.
- **Experiment efficiency:** outcome improvement or target attainment relative to interventions consumed; exact definition and endpoint are for DAL-120/preregistration.
- **Failure and invalid-action behavior:** counts/rates and types of invalid actions, parsing/provider failures, budget violations, timeouts, and incomplete runs. Failures remain data; exclusions require declared rules.
- **Variance:** outcome variability across worlds and across stochastic repeats within worlds, reported separately where the design permits.
- **Operational cost and latency:** model calls, tokens and monetary cost when available, and elapsed/call latency under a declared measurement procedure.

Exact formulas, normalization, aggregation, primary endpoint, and uncertainty method are not fixed here; they must be selected before confirmatory runs. Do not use chain-of-thought or hidden model reasoning as a dependent variable.

## Controls and comparison

- **Seeded-random policy:** selects among valid experimental choices using its own explicit policy-randomness stream. This estimates performance of a non-adaptive stochastic selection strategy and guards against attributing generic exploration or favorable chance to the LLM.
- **Fixed non-adaptive design:** uses a design specified before confirmatory evaluation, without adapting choices to observations. This provides a reproducible non-adaptive reference and prevents an LLM result from being judged only against a weak random baseline.

Control design quality matters. The fixed design must be defensible and declared before confirmatory outcomes; its design must not be selected using confirmatory truth or results. The LLM and controls receive matched worlds, budgets, action constraints, public information, and declared observation conditions. Where feasible, world assignments should be paired across policies to reduce irrelevant between-world variation. The choice of fixed design and pairing/randomization method remain to be documented before the sweep.

## Budget and failure accounting

The primary resource budget is the number of accepted, budget-consuming interventions that produce or request a measurement. The benchmark must declare whether an attempted invalid action consumes an intervention; to avoid a hidden advantage, the default protocol decision is that invalid attempts consume a decision opportunity and are recorded, with no extra unbudgeted opportunity to replace them. Any departure must be applied consistently across policies and frozen before evaluation.

Model calls, wall-clock time, and monetary spend do not silently buy additional interventions. Provider retries, parser repair, and action correction are not free: retry behavior, maximum calls, and whether any retry consumes a decision opportunity must be fixed before confirmatory evaluation and recorded. No policy receives extra experiments because it failed to return a valid action. Termination before budget exhaustion is recorded as a policy decision or failure as appropriate; unused budget is not reassigned. Operational resources should be reported separately from intervention budget.

## Randomness and reproducibility

Maintain conceptually and operationally separate randomness for:

- **World generation:** determines hidden physical truth/configuration.
- **Measurement noise:** determines observation perturbations, independently of world generation.
- **Baseline policy:** drives seeded-random action selection, independently of world and noise.
- **Stochastic model runs:** represented by repeat identity and model sampling configuration; provider sampling need not be reproducible from a local seed, so preserve request settings and returned records.

Do not collapse these streams into a global seed. Seed values and mappings are evaluator/provenance data and must not be exposed to the policy. Persist enough provenance to replay deterministic components or independently analyze a run. Confirmatory seed sets must be held out from prompt/protocol tuning.

## Pilot and confirmatory phases

Pilot experiments may debug the apparatus and schemas, assess action-space usability, estimate operational cost, select plausible noise regimes and sample sizes, inspect metric saturation, and refine prompts or protocol details. Pilot results are exploratory. Pilot worlds/seeds and their outcomes must not silently enter confirmatory estimates; keep their artifacts and labels distinct.

Before confirmatory execution, freeze and version the task definition, policy/model identity and settings, prompts, public information, controls, budgets, noise conditions, outcomes/metrics, sample size, analysis, exclusions, retry rules, and confirmatory seed set. Confirmatory execution begins only after those choices are recorded. A material post-start change requires a new protocol version/experiment designation; do not continue presenting altered runs as the same confirmatory study.

## Threats to validity and safeguards

| Threat | Why it matters | Safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| Simulator artifacts or numerical error | Policies may exploit implementation quirks rather than learn physical behavior. | Validate numerical behavior against analytical/limiting cases where available; record solver settings; keep artifacts distinct from intended task properties. |
| Benchmark leakage | Hidden truth, seed structure, labels, or evaluator data can make inference appear successful without experimentation. | Enforce the threat model in `leakage-threat-model.md`; test serialized prompts, errors, paths, logs, and shared structures. |
| Overly weak controls | Beating random alone may not show useful adaptivity. | Include both seeded-random and predeclared fixed design; justify the latter before confirmation. |
| Prompt sensitivity | Small wording changes may reverse results. | Freeze/version prompts before confirmation; treat post-hoc prompt changes as exploratory/new conditions. |
| Stochastic model outputs | A small number of runs can confound policy quality with sampling variation. | Declare repeats, sampling configuration, and uncertainty analysis; report run-to-run variance. |
| Metric choice | Selective endpoints or unsuitable normalization can favor a policy. | Declare primary and secondary metrics before confirmation; retain raw artifacts and report uncertainty and failures. |
| Environment-specific conclusions | A single oscillator cannot support claims about scientific reasoning generally. | Bound conclusions to the tested oscillator distribution and conditions; generalization is a later study. |
| Hidden retry/resource advantages | Extra calls or repaired actions can give a policy more effective opportunities. | Freeze retry accounting and per-run resource caps; log attempts and failures. |
| Small sample sizes | Estimates may be unstable and miss meaningful heterogeneity. | Determine world count/repeats before confirmation with a justified precision/power rationale; show uncertainty and avoid overclaiming. |
| API/model drift | A provider may change the model or serving behavior during a sweep. | Record provider, exact model/version where available, settings, timestamps, and failures; define in advance how unavailable/changed models are handled. |

## Observable decision trace

Record the sequence of public state presented, structured action/estimate emitted, validation outcome, observation returned, remaining budget, call/retry/failure status, and timing/cost metadata available under the protocol. Preserve model/provider identity and prompt/configuration version. Do not request or score private chain-of-thought; a concise structured rationale is optional only if explicitly treated as an experimental variable and must not determine scientific correctness.

## Future system-one experiment

V0 should leave the ordinary policy decision boundary observable so a later fast-model advisor could be inserted before a primary policy decision without changing the physical task or privileged access. The advisor is inactive in V0 and V0.1. A future controlled study must compare advisor-disabled and advisor-enabled conditions with otherwise matched resources and must permit positive, null, or harmful effects, including increased cost, latency, variance, or anchoring. This is a future experimental question, not a design assumption.

## Decisions intentionally unresolved

The following require scientific justification and preregistration rather than invention here: oscillator parameter distributions/ranges; forcing and action domains; exact public task information; held-out prediction design; noise scales/regimes; budget values; fixed-design selection; primary endpoint and metric formulas; world/repeat sample sizes; statistical analysis and uncertainty procedure; retry accounting details; and confirmatory seed set.
