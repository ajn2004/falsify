# DAL-202 structural-inference design decision v1

**FAIL — no currently validated structural candidate justifies progression to
DAL-156.** This is a completed negative apparatus decision, not a claim that
structural-inference research is impossible. It is not a treatment result.
No environment semantics, FixedDesignPolicy, frozen V0.1 behavior/artifacts,
LLM treatment, confirmatory seed, or preregistration lock was changed/run.

## Evidence and what it establishes

Structural v1/v2 failed because a high-energy, low-frequency legal action acted
as a one-action discriminator on small exploratory samples. V1 FixedDesign
classified 12/12 sampled worlds across tested budgets/noise; v2's policy-free
map found a single action correct on 16/16 replicate-majority worlds. These are
not population guarantees. V2's later poor Duffing recovery under generic
designs was class-asymmetric recoverability, not proof of optimizer failure or
universal Duffing unidentifiability: the action map contained both ambiguous
and discriminating controls, with fits converging.

V3 capped drive amplitude. Its action map again showed ambiguous and
discriminating interventions and converged candidate fits. The finite-budget
diagnostic used 16 independent, balanced validation worlds (8 per class),
three independent noise realizations per world/design, and budgets 1/2/4/8.
One diverse precommitted schedule classified all 8/8 linear and 8/8 Duffing
worlds by budget 4. A compact, reproducible audit is retained at
`results/pilot-v0.2/structural-gate-audit-v1/diagnostic.json`. It distinguishes
16 physical worlds from 48 RandomPolicy world-design combinations; the latter
are repeated designs on the same worlds, not 48 independent worlds.

### Budget endpoints and uncertainty

For the strong diverse open-loop schedule, each cell below reports correct
classification over 8 distinct worlds/class; 95% intervals are Wilson
intervals based on those eight physical worlds. They are intentionally wide.
The confusion counts count world-design outcomes after a 3-noise-replicate
majority. RandomPolicy needs a different estimator because it has three
independently sampled schedules per world: results for that estimator are
reported separately below.

| Budget | Linear correct / 8 (95% CI) | Duffing correct / 8 (95% CI) | Median selected held-out RMSE |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 8/8 (0.676–1.000) | 4/8 (0.215–0.785) | 0.00626 m |
| 2 | 8/8 (0.676–1.000) | 5/8 (0.306–0.863) | 0.00470 m |
| 4 | 8/8 (0.676–1.000) | 8/8 (0.676–1.000) | 0.00293 m |
| 8 | 8/8 (0.676–1.000) | 8/8 (0.676–1.000) | 0.00299 m |

The RMSE median aggregates converged observation rows from three noise
replicates, then across worlds/designs; it is not a world-level independent
sample or an adaptive-vs-open-loop effect. Across budgets 1/2/4/8, Duffing
correct counts for FixedDesign were 2/2/3/3 of 8; the single repeated
max-low-frequency schedule had 3/6/5/6 of 8. Each selected linear in 8/8
linear worlds at every budget. Wilson intervals for these one-design policies
use eight distinct worlds and are in the audit artifact. The purpose is not to
claim the diverse schedule is universally superior; it shows a credible fixed
schedule can saturate class correctness in the observed sample. All v3 fits in
the source budget diagnostic converged. The v3 budget records do not
store fitted parameter estimates, so v3 parameter-estimation quality was not
compared here. See the derived artifact for all policies, class-conditional
confusion, Wilson intervals, observation counts, and held-out prediction
summaries.

For RandomPolicy, each sampled schedule is scored separately using its
3-noise-replicate majority. We calculate each world's fraction of its three
schedule replicates that selected the true class, then average those fractions
equally across the 8 Duffing worlds. This estimates single-schedule performance
without treating the 24 schedule draws as independent worlds. The resulting
mean correctness fractions at budgets 1/2/4/8 were 0.000, 0.125, 0.417, and
0.625; 95% percentile intervals from a deterministic 10,000-draw physical-
world cluster bootstrap were [0,0], [0.042,0.250], [0.250,0.542], and
[0.458,0.792]. Linear correctness was 1.0 at each budget. For comparison, the
majority across three RandomPolicy schedules classified 0/8, 0/8, 3/8, and 5/8
Duffing worlds correctly. That is retained as a **descriptive schedule-ensemble
diagnostic only**, not an outcome from one RandomPolicy schedule. The artifact
reports both estimands and their distinct aggregation levels.

Held-out prediction and conditional parameter estimation remain relevant
endpoints: both can retain headroom after class accuracy saturates, and wrong
class selection must not be hidden by a selected-model metric. The v3 records
contain selected-model held-out errors, but do not preserve fitted parameters
or conditional parameter errors. Thus parameter quality for this schedule
comparison remains unmeasured. No analysis demonstrates observation-conditioned
improvement on prediction or parameter quality. It would be unjustified to
select smaller budget 1/2 solely because a fixed schedule has lower accuracy
there.

As separate historical evidence (structural v2, σ=0.05 m, 16 balanced worlds;
not pooled with v3), pilot v6 reported these median errors by budget:

| Policy | Endpoint | B1 | B2 | B4 | B8 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Random | Selected held-out RMSE (m) | 0.0247 | 0.0118 | 0.0085 | 0.0070 |

The conditional parameter metric is defined only when the selected class is

are class-conditional and not directly comparable as an all-world endpoint.
This v2 evidence shows finite-budget prediction/parameter changes, not a
sequential advantage or a reason to change v3's budget.

## Scientific alternatives

### A. Measurement bandwidth and cost

The current measurement is 101 equally spaced observations over 10 seconds
(0.1-second cadence) with independent additive Gaussian displacement noise
σ=0.05 m. Fewer or sparser samples could discard transient curvature, phase,
decay-rate, or resonance information; the resulting identifiability change
depends on which samples are removed and on the noise/covariance model. It
could also alter the effective information per intervention and the BIC sample
count. No v1/v2/v3 comparison varies bandwidth or cadence, and there is no
physical sensor-acquisition cost model. Thus the existing evidence neither
shows 101 samples are excessive nor justifies choosing a shorter window,
sparser cadence, or arbitrary cost. Revisit only with a defensible measurement
process and equal total acquisition-cost comparison.

### B. Budget and endpoints

The budget evidence above covers 1/2/4/8, both classes, finite noise, and
multiple policies. It shows an open-loop schedule can reach sample ceiling by
budget 4; it does not show the population ceiling or establish an adaptive
advantage at smaller budgets. Class correctness, held-out prediction, and
class-conditional parameter error are distinct endpoints. Prediction RMSE
falls from budget 1 to 4 for the diverse schedule in this sample, with a small
increase at 8; that descriptive pattern is not evidence of adaptivity. A
future comparison must predeclare endpoint hierarchy and report uncertainty
over independent worlds, not count design/noise replicates as additional
worlds.

### C. Model-family redesign

The current linear-vs-Duffing pair has nested structural signatures: the cubic
restoring contribution `βx³` is small relative to the linear term at low
amplitude, but a fixed legal high-amplitude/low-frequency schedule accumulates
enough evidence to separate the sampled classes. The same broad set of legal
controls and 101-point trajectories therefore lets a precommitted schedule
cover the useful signature before there is a demonstrated need to adapt.

One physically motivated next candidate is **linear viscous damping versus
quadratic drag** (`F_d=-c₂|v|v`), with a shared restoring law and class-
neutral controls. At low velocities the terms can be confounded with effective
linear damping; velocity reversals and high-speed portions may expose
amplitude-dependent energy loss. Hidden damping scale and resonance/decay
regime could make a first noisy decay trace useful for choosing between a
high-velocity transient and a near-resonant forced experiment. This mechanism
is a hypothesis only: neither physical supports, identifiable parameterization,
numerical behavior, nor adaptive value is validated here. Do not search
unrestrictedly for a model pair that makes FixedDesign fail.

## Adaptation: measured versus unmeasured

1. **Oracle action rankings conditioned on hidden truth:** measured in v8/v3
   nuisance-refit maps. Action evidence/recoverability varies with truth and
   control. This is not realizable adaptation.
2. **Action rankings inferred from noisy public observations:** not measured.
   Existing action maps fit individual noisy interventions against evaluator
   truth; the finite-budget policy comparisons are precommitted or random. No
   noisy first public observation was used to select the next action.
3. **Expected adaptive information gain or held-out-loss reduction over an
   equally costly open-loop design:** not measured. No conditional expected
   information/loss estimator or paired equal-cost adaptive diagnostic exists.

Consequently the current experiments support a finite-sample failure of the
candidate's intended selection challenge, not population-wide impossibility of
adaptive value. The v3 four-action schedule saturating 16 worlds is a valid
stop-gate signal; it is not a theorem about other worlds, endpoints, or
physical model families.

## Stop decision, successor, and DAL-156 handoff

**FAIL — no currently validated structural candidate justifies progression to
DAL-156.** Stop structural redesign here. Do not implement v4, tune noise or
budget to weaken baselines, alter FixedDesign, use LLM outcomes, run treatments,
execute confirmation, or generate a lock. Existing v1/v2/v3 semantics and
artifacts remain historical records.

The narrowly scoped successor should specify and test one physical model-family
pair (recommendation: linear viscous versus quadratic drag) before environment
implementation. Required gates: explicit competing hypotheses and shared
class-neutral controls/measurement semantics; defensible nuisance confounding;
noisy public first-observation diagnostics; evidence observations change the
rational next experiment; expected information or held-out-loss comparison to
strong fixed/open-loop schedules at equal costs; independent class-balanced
validation; bounded nuisance fits and uncertainty; prospective PASS/FAIL stop
conditions. A failed candidate ends the successor without another automatic
model-family search.

DAL-156 block remains. DAL-156's operational/reliability gate remains
independently required; DAL-157 stays downstream of DAL-156. Completion of this
negative decision does not unblock either ticket.

## Reproduction and provenance

From repository root:

```sh
julia +1.12.7 --project=. scripts/v0_2_structural_gate_audit.jl
julia +1.12.7 --project=. test/runtests.jl
julia +1.12.7 --project=. scripts/v0.2_design_checks.jl
julia +1.12.7 --project=. -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.test()'
```

The audit validates the exact declared Cartesian product of source-manifest
strategies, physical-world seeds, design replicate IDs, budgets, and noise
replicate IDs; it rejects missing and unexpected combinations. It preserves
fit failures in confusion outcomes, reports physical-world uncertainty (and
the world-clustered RandomPolicy single-schedule estimator), and separately
labels prediction-RMSE aggregation. It writes deterministic JSON with the
source artifact SHA-256, seed provenance, environment/evaluator metadata, and
audit version. Unit tests use small synthetic data; one test verifies the
committed derived output against re-generation from the retained source file.
